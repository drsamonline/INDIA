Agra · Mughal · 1632–1653

Taj Mahal

ताज महल

Shah Jahan commissioned the Taj Mahal in 1631, the year his wife Mumtaz Mahal died in childbirth at Burhanpur. The tomb stands on the right bank of the Yamuna at Agra, at the centre of a 17-hectare complex holding a mosque, a guest house and a formal four-part garden inside a crenellated wall.

Construction of the mausoleum was finished in 1648 and the rest of the complex about five years later. The building is clad in translucent white marble from Makrana in Rajasthan, inlaid with lapis lazuli, carnelian, jade and jasper in floral pietra dura, and framed by black marble calligraphy, scaled so that it reads evenly from the ground.

The dome rises about 35 metres above its drum, crowned by a gilded finial, with four chhatris and four minarets leaning slightly outward. The marble shifts colour through the day: pink at dawn, white at noon, gold at dusk and silver under the moon. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.
